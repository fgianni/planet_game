## Mesh and advection (ADR-0002, M5--M8)

**Santos, L. F., Granjeiro, J. B. & Peixoto, P. S., *A new high-order
finite-volume advection scheme on spherical Voronoi grids and a comparative
study in a mimetic finite-volume moist shallow-water model*.** arXiv
`2604.07103`. Preprint; venue and date not stated on the page, reference list
runs to 2026.

The closest published match to our mesh: spherical centroidal Voronoi
tessellations generated by Lloyd's method from an icosahedron, with TRiSK
mimetic C-grid momentum and depth, and a moist shallow-water model on top.

- Extends Ollivier-Gooch k-exact least-squares reconstruction to the sphere:
  per-cell reconstruction on a local tangent plane, least-squares fit
  preserving the cell averages of the stencil, Gaussian quadrature with one
  or two points per edge, upwind-biased. Gives OG2/OG3/OG4.
- Compared against Skamarock & Gassmann (2011). OG4 reaches ~3.5 order and
  the smallest errors in pure advection; SG4 is unstable without flux
  limiting. Three-stage Runge-Kutta with a Zalesak limiter on the last stage.
- Cost: OG3 ~10% over SG3 at levels 6--7; OG4 ~1% over OG3. SG2 cheapest.
  Timings are from research laptops with Fortran/OpenMP, so treat as
  indicative only.

**[OPEN PROBLEM] Grid imprinting.** The paper reports that imprinting
persists in tracer fields *even with high-order advection*, that errors are
larger near pentagonal cells, and that it attributes this partly to the
low-order TRiSK discretization of fluid depth entering the tracer flux form.
It concludes the advection scheme alone cannot remove the effect.

This supports ADR-0002 §9's decision to **measure and bound** the pentagon
signature rather than try to remove it, and it tells us where to look if the
bound is ever exceeded: the depth/thickness discretization, not the advection
operator. Note one inconsistency in the paper itself — the abstract claims
insensitivity to grid distortion while the conclusions report that the
second- and fourth-order schemes are more affected by it.

**Weller, H., Thuburn, J. & Cotter, C. J., *Computational modes and grid
imprinting on five quasi-uniform spherical C-grids*.** Monthly Weather
Review. Canonical reference for the trade-offs between hexagonal, cubed-
sphere and triangular C-grids; read before changing anything about the dual.

**MPAS / MPAS-Ocean.** Open source, SCVT meshes, TRiSK discretization. The
nearest production code to our mesh and a credible validation oracle. The
local time-stepping work is relevant to specification §8: *Conservative
explicit local time-stepping schemes for the shallow water equations*
(J. Comput. Phys.) shows how to run different regions at different rates
while conserving mass — which is our multi-rate requirement with our
conservation constraint.

## Atmosphere and heat transport (M5--M7, §23)

**[OPEN PROBLEM] Feldl, N. & Merlis, T. M. (2021), *Polar amplification in
idealized climates: the role of ice, moisture, and seasons*.** Geophysical
Research Letters 48, e2021GL094130.

Our polar amplification sits at ×1.6--2.1 against a §23 target of ×2--4, and
land warms barely faster than ocean (×1.03). This paper is the most direct
lead on both.

- Removing **latent** heat transport from the diffusive atmospheric energy
  transport reduces polar amplification **by a factor of 1.8**, across the
  whole range of ice representations tested. Ice and moist-transport effects
  are described as nearly additive.
- Thermodynamic sea ice (as against a plain temperature-dependent albedo)
  changes the *seasonal pattern* of polar warming and modestly *reduces*
  annual-mean amplification.
- The seasonal cycle of insolation shapes the response strongly: weak warming
  in the warm season (thin ice absorbs little extra sunlight), large
  radiatively forced warming in the cold season over thick ice.

Implication for us: `D_HEAT` is a single dry diffusion. Splitting transport
into dry and latent (moist static energy) components is the first thing to
try, and it is consistent with the §23 calibration lesson that transport
processes are cross-coupled — the split must be accompanied by recalibration
of `D_HEAT` and `ADV_OCEAN` together. The seasonal findings are a second,
independent argument for the explicit seasonal cycle decided in
specification §8.

**Williams et al. (2026), *State-dependence of polar amplification in an
idealized GCM*.** Geophysical Research Letters, `10.1029/2025GL118423`. Not
yet read in detail; relevant if amplification turns out to depend on the base
state we calibrate at.

## Clouds (M8, §9.4, and the Godot client)

Two separate problems that must not be conflated: the **cloud fraction** the
radiation code needs, and the **cloud volume** the renderer draws.

### Simulation side: cloud fraction

**ETH Zürich, *Parameterization of large-scale clouds*, Weather and Climate
Models course, FS2024.** Lecture notes, freely available, and the most
directly implementable summary found. Starts from the right premise: there is
no governing equation for cloud fraction, so every scheme is a closure.

- **Diagnostic RH (Sundqvist-type):**
  `CC = 1 - sqrt((1 - RH) / (1 - RH_crit))`, with
  `RH = min(1, max(RH, RH_crit))` and `RH_crit` typically 60--80%, highest
  near the surface to avoid excessive fog. Simplest usable form for a coarse
  model. **Known failure mode: "cloud blinking"** — high-frequency on/off
  behaviour — which is exactly the artefact to expect if we drive the
  renderer from a diagnostic per-cell fraction.
- **Statistical / PDF:** assume a distribution `G(q)` of total water in the
  cell; `CC = ∫_{q_sat}^{∞} G(q) dq`. For a top-hat PDF of width `2Δq` this
  collapses to `CC = (q + Δq - q_sat) / (2Δq)`, equivalent to the RH form for
  fixed `Δq`. Avoids blinking, gives fraction and condensate consistently,
  and `Δq` can come from the turbulence scheme.
- **Prognostic (Tiedtke 1993):** cloud cover advected with the flow, with
  sources (grid-scale transport and diffusion, convection, boundary-layer
  turbulence, stratiform condensation) and sinks (evaporation, precipitation
  generation, cloud-top entrainment). Carries memory; costs many process
  terms.

Recommendation for M8: start from the top-hat PDF form rather than the bare
RH form. It is the same cost, avoids blinking, and yields condensate
consistently — which §9.4 needs anyway.

### Rendering side: cloud volume

**Loboda, M., Bohak, C., Marolt, M. & Lesar, Ž. (2025), *Real-time
volumetric cloud rendering for games and simulations*.** ERK 2025, Portorož,
pp. 337--340.

Matches our architecture almost exactly, including its final recommendation
to **drive the weather map from an external runtime** — which is what
PlanetSim is.

- A 2D weather map holds coverage in R and cloud type in B, with type
  selecting a height gradient. Our advected coverage field feeds R; a
  convective/stratiform split feeds B.
- 128³ base texture (Perlin-Worley plus low-frequency inverse Worley,
  combined by fBm), 32³ high-frequency Worley to erode edges, linear height
  gradient to shape the layer. Wind by scrolling both 3D noise textures.
- Single scattering with a Henyey-Greenstein phase function, plus a
  deliberately non-physical ambient term from local density.
- Practical: adaptive stepping (long steps through empty space, refine on
  density, back to long steps after several empty samples; shorten less
  aggressively for near-horizontal rays); clip rays to the layer's min/max
  heights; cheap density sample before the expensive detail sample; thinner
  layers are much cheaper; expose quality presets.
- 2.6 ms at 1440p on an RX 7900 XT. Aliasing inside the volume is unsolved,
  and no temporal reprojection is described — only noise scrolling.

Caveat for us: our art direction is a small Spore-like planet viewed from
outside, so full in-volume raymarching may be more than we need. The weather-
map contract is the part to keep regardless.

Also: **Ford, P. (2024), *Real-time rendering of atmospheric clouds***,
MSc thesis, University of Washington — longer treatment of the same ground if
the ERK paper is too compressed.

## Rivers and drainage (M2-03, M9, ADR-0005)

**Barnes, R., Lehman, C. & Mulla, D. (2014), *Priority-Flood: an optimal
depression-filling and watershed-labeling algorithm for digital elevation
models*.** Computers & Geosciences 62.

This is the algorithm ADR-0005's drainage needs, and it is explicitly
suitable for our mesh: the paper states it "can work on irregular meshes as
well as 4-, 6-, 8-, and n-connected grids", that the grid "need not even be
rectangular", and that it "does not rely on the structure or connectedness of
the underlying DEM".

| Variant | Purpose | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Alg. 1 Priority-Flood | depression filling via priority queue | O(n) integer; O(n log n) float |
| Alg. 2 Improved | plain queue for cells inside depressions | O(n) integer; O(m log m), m ≤ n, float |
| Alg. 3 Priority-Flood+ | fills while preserving a small gradient to the outlet; no flats | — |
| Alg. 4 +FlowDirs | flow directions by carving through depression walls | plain-queue speedup unavailable |
| Alg. 5 | watershed labelling | — |

- Use **Alg. 2** for float elevations; it was 12--37% faster than Alg. 1 in
  their tests.
- Use **Alg. 3** if we need flow directions with no flat regions (it needs a
  total-order priority queue and `NextAfter`).
- Reference C++ implementation is under 100 lines:
  `github.com/r-barnes/Barnes2013-Depressions`. Also in RichDEM.
- Deterministic given a fixed cell ordering, which we have from ADR-0002's
  recursive ordering — so this satisfies ADR-0003 L0 without special care.

**Landlab** (CSDMS). Open-source landscape-evolution framework with
`HexModelGrid` and Voronoi/Delaunay grid classes plus stream-power and
erosion-deposition components. Best available **validation oracle** for
M2-03 and M9: build the equivalent hex-grid case in Landlab and compare
drainage areas and channel profiles before trusting our own.

**Frozen Fractal, *Around the World, part 23: hydraulic erosion* (June
2025).** Practitioner devlog, valuable mainly for its honest failure
accounting on a project much like ours.

- The Mei et al. pipe model did not work for the author: axis-aligned
  branching artefacts, instability, ~10 parameters to tune.
- The stream-power law `E = K·A^m·S^n` with iteratively accumulated drainage
  area left many inland lakes below sea level — endorheic basins, because the
  law only applies to *flowing* water. This is precisely the failure
  Priority-Flood exists to prevent, and it is worth a test of its own.
- The adopted method is one droplet per cell with merging and evaporation:
  20 iterations under 1.5 s on 2048×1024.
- Note the author moved *off* the sphere to a plane for the rewrite, so the
  geometry lessons do not transfer; the failure modes do.

**Tzathas, P. et al. (2024), *Physically-based analytical erosion for fast
terrain generation*.** Computer Graphics Forum, `10.1111/cgf.15033`.
Analytical rather than iterative; worth reading if M2's diffusive
approximation proves too slow at L6.

## Ocean (M11, §23 AMOC rows, §13.3)

**[OPEN PROBLEM] Mehling, O., Vanderborght, E. & Dijkstra, H. A. (2026),
*Critical freshwater forcing for AMOC tipping in climate models --
compensation matters*.** Earth System Dynamics 17, 563--579, published
2026-05-12.

Gives us both a test protocol and a conservation warning.

- **Protocol (quasi-equilibrium hosing):** apply freshwater as a virtual salt
  flux over 20--50° N, ramp at 0.3 Sv/kyr for 1500 years to 0.45 Sv, then
  *reduce* the forcing until the AMOC recovers, then ramp back up to close
  the hysteresis loop. Critical forcing is taken as the point where the AMOC
  reaches 7 Sv. This is directly reusable as the recipe for the §23 rows on
  weakening, collapse and hysteresis, and for specification M13's sustained-
  crossing vs brief-overshoot pair.
- **The warning:** *how global salinity is held fixed moves the tipping
  point.* Surface compensation shifts tipping to higher forcing than volume
  compensation by about 0.06 Sv in both POP (1°) and Climber-X, shrinking to
  ~0.04 Sv at a slower ramp but staying qualitatively robust. On the Off
  branch, POP recovers ~0.2 Sv earlier under volume compensation.
  Translation for us: the freshwater/salt budget is not a detail we can treat
  loosely. `RAIN_TO_FRESH` and the two-box salinity need a closed,
  conservative budget or the threshold is an artefact of the leak.
- They also reconstruct the AMOC from the meridional density gradient via
  thermal wind balance to attribute pre-tipping changes — a cheap diagnostic
  we can compute.
- The paper does **not** discuss critical-slowing-down indicators, and argues
  the present-day distance to tipping may be overestimated in recent studies.

**van Westen, R. M. et al. (2025), *Physics-based indicators for the onset of
an AMOC collapse under climate change*.** JGR Oceans,
`10.1029/2025JC022651`. Could not be retrieved (publisher error) — **not yet
read.** Relevant to specification §13.3, which currently specifies generic
lag-1 autocorrelation and variance; physics-based indicators would likely be
both earlier and cheaper. Worth fetching before M11 lands.

**Noise-shaped hysteresis cycles of the AMOC under increasing CO2 forcing.**
Chaos 35, 023167 (2025). Hysteresis under noise — bears on the §23 row where
a brief overshoot may recover, since noise changes where that boundary sits.
Not yet read.

**Also unread, for later:** *Structural stability changes of the AMOC* (npj
Climate and Atmospheric Science, 2025); *Physics of AMOC multistable regime
shifts due to freshwater biases in an EMIC* (Earth System Dynamics 16, 1221,
2025); *Assessing AMOC stability using a Bayesian nested time-dependent
autoregressive model* (Nonlinear Processes in Geophysics 32, 383, 2025).

## How to use this file

- Nothing here overrides an accepted decision record. Where a reference and
  an ADR conflict, the ADR wins and the conflict goes in the ADR's
  consequences section.
- A reference adopted into the code is cited in the code comment by author,
  year and equation or section number, and added to the milestone's task
  document.
- Entries marked "not yet read" are leads, not authority. Do not cite them
  as support for a decision.
