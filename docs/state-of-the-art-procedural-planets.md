# State of the art: procedural planets and terrain

**The most urgent rendering question is how to keep one shoreline stable through terrain LOD transitions; the most promising later generator experiment is drainage-aware erosion. Neither has a drop-in paper solution.** Losasso and Hoppe offer a registered multiresolution mechanism to investigate for the first; Cordonnier et al. offer an offline uplift–erosion model for the second. Cortial et al. remains the closest geological-history model, while Derzapf and Grenier offer constrained visual detail rather than authoritative rivers. The simulator already has a committed hexagonal–pentagonal spherical Voronoi mesh, nine-quantile hypsometry, connected-ocean fractional coasts and cell-mesh drainage; replacing those foundations with a paper’s triangle mesh, binary shoreline or learned heightmap would violate accepted decisions. The measured coarse-to-detailed coastline jump requires an ADR-0022 amendment, not an inference from a clipmap paper. This assessment reflects accessible primary sources and repository contracts as of **2026-10-24**; authoring studies establish appearance or engineering feasibility, not Earth-system validity.

## Scope and evidence: appearance is not physical validation

The ranking below weights **transfer to this project’s present bottlenecks**, source quality and architectural fit, rather than publication recency or screenshots. The primary evidence is the cited papers, author-hosted manuscripts and, where a full text was inaccessible, explicitly limited abstracts; implementation facts and gates come from the [development specification](DEVELOPMENT_SPEC_v0_8.md), [ADR-0002](decisions/0002-mesh-and-field-layout.md), [ADR-0005](decisions/0005-coastlines-and-drainage.md), [ADR-0018](decisions/0018-presentation-channels-and-style-packs.md), [ADR-0022](decisions/0022-player-placement-zoning-and-continuous-zoom.md) and [R1-05 investigation](tasks/R1-05-coastline-lod-stability.md). A visual preference, breaching score or historical frame rate cannot demonstrate conserved runoff, correct ocean connectivity, deterministic replay or a stable georeferenced shore. Earth-like distributions are useful controlled references, not mandatory aesthetics for every scenario. The project’s [references catalogue](references.md) adds Barnes’s Priority-Flood and Landlab as drainage-method and comparison leads; it does not override accepted ADRs.

## The current architecture already separates geology from depiction

**PlanetSim’s simulation grid is fixed by ADR-0002:** an icosahedron-derived centroidal spherical Voronoi dual, with 10,242 cells at L5 and 40,962 at L6, including twelve pentagons. The completed M2 generator grows connected spherical plates, assigns Euler motions and crust attributes, classifies relative boundary motion, forms uplift/ridges/trenches, adds multiscale roughness and diffusive smoothing, then samples sub-cell elevations ([M2-02](tasks/M2-02-plates-terrain-and-sea-level.md); [specification §13, M2](DEVELOPMENT_SPEC_v0_8.md)). It is **not** Cortial’s geological-time plate-evolution engine. The authoritative slow terrain is nine ordered elevation quantiles per cell and one global sea level, not a displayed DEM or an independent mean-height field. Ocean fraction follows those quantiles only in the connected component anchored at the deepest cell; disconnected below-sea ground stays dry pending lake hydrology. Derived drainage uses deterministic priority-flood and one downstream edge-neighbour per non-outlet land cell; catchment area is static geometry, **not discharge**. M9 runoff, rivers and lakes have not begun ([ADR-0005 §§4, 9–10](decisions/0005-coastlines-and-drainage.md)).

The read-only path is `StateSnapshot/TerrainSnapshot → sim/presentation → VisualFrame → Godot`: styles consume semantic channels, and rendering must not change state or PSNAP ([ADR-0018 §4](decisions/0018-presentation-channels-and-style-packs.md)). Under ADR-0022, the camera continuously zooms across globe, region and local bands; a cube-sphere quadtree streams tiles sampled from world-seed- and position-keyed detail, with camera-relative precision in stock Godot. The detailed relief maps a positional noise rank through each cell’s quantile curve and blends toward shared corner curves at boundaries; V4 bounds area–elevation mismatch, while V9–V13 cover tile flight, path independence, performance and jitter ([ADR-0022 §§4.8–5](decisions/0022-player-placement-zoning-and-continuous-zoom.md)). Yet coarse tiles use a filtered mean-height surface while detailed tiles use rank relief. The proposed, **unimplemented** R1-05 reports that, for one L4 coastal seed and 532 samples, 100 samples changed land/water classification at that handoff. This is a measured presentation failure, not a change in authoritative land fraction; it is neither an L6 population estimate nor proof that one particular remedy works ([R1-05](tasks/R1-05-coastline-lod-stability.md)).

## Ranked papers: borrow mechanisms, not their meshes or claims

**1 — Cortial et al. (2019), “Procedural Tectonic Planets,”** is the closest *planet-generation* reference. Its spherical, time-stepped plates track crust type, thickness and oceanic age as well as orogen age/type and preferred fold directions; spreading, subduction, continental collision and rifting leave reusable regional attributes. The transferable idea is **history as structured generator metadata**—for example inherited ridge orientation, passive-margin history or orogen age for later lithology and resources—rather than noise as the whole cause of height. Its fine relief is procedural or exemplar-based, and the authors explicitly do not claim geophysical prediction or continental drainage generation. Their matched-shading preference study supports perceptual plausibility, not mass balance; its pooled votes should not be mistaken for independent participant-level replication. Investigate incremental metadata and conditional boundary morphology only when a consumer warrants them; do not replace today’s authoritative quantiles with a second elevation field or run million-year tectonics during gameplay ([primary manuscript §§3–5, 7](https://hal.science/hal-02136820v1/file/2019-Procedural-Tectonic-Planets.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.13614)).

**2 — Cordonnier et al. (2016), “Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion,”** offers the highest-value *offline process* experiment. Prescribed uplift interacts with a single-receiver drainage tree and stream-power incision, `∂h/∂t = u − k A^m s^n`; the paper reconnects internal lakes through passes and iterates flow and implicit height updates. Here plate-boundary uplift and the existing cell-neighbour graph are the inputs, and candidate incision must be applied **before** deterministic sub-cell resampling, sea-level solve, ocean-connectivity check and drainage rebuild. Its random planar triangulation, separate outlet graph, lake supergraph and regional timing do not transfer wholesale to a spherical L6 authority. Its visual results do not establish an Earth-calibrated erodibility or a sediment budget ([primary manuscript §§3–7](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonier16CGF.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12820)). For independent routing checks, Barnes et al.’s irregular-mesh-capable [Priority-Flood](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2013.04.024) and [Landlab](https://landlab.readthedocs.io/) are method/oracle leads, not reasons to discard ADR-0005’s implemented deterministic tie and spill rules.

**3 — Losasso and Hoppe (2004), “Geometry Clipmaps,”** is the clearest *LOD-transition* mechanism. It builds registered, prefiltered levels of **one** heightfield and morphs corresponding coarse and fine heights across a transition; crack repair and coordinated texture transitions are separate operations. That distinction maps directly onto the project’s mean-surface/rank-surface handoff: test a band-limited hierarchy and coherent height, water-colour and normal transitions without necessarily adopting viewer-centred planar clipmap rings. Crucially, morphing can still move or create a sea-level contour during intermediate phases, and the paper flags coastline colour as a sensitive exception to otherwise unobtrusive compression. It cannot infer a unique geographic coast from nine cell-wise quantiles ([authors’ paper §§1, 6–7](https://hhoppe.com/geomclipmap.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1015706.1015799)). **Kooima et al. (2009)** ranks next for composition of georeferenced, heterogeneous planetary heights/imagery and out-of-core precision; its registered-data premise is useful for a chosen coast representation, but its author’s dissertation says continuous geometry LOD/geomorphing was not demonstrated in that implementation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2009.43); [dissertation §§3.2.5, 3.3.6](https://www.evl.uic.edu/documents/kooima-dissertation-uic.pdf)). Clasen and Hege’s [spherical clipmaps](https://malteclasen.de/zib/index5534.html?page_id=6) are a spherical mapping precedent, not a shoreline-topology proof.

**4 — Derzapf et al. (2011), “River Networks for Instant Procedural Planets,”** supplies adaptive *render-geometry* rules: reproduce splits regardless of camera traversal, keep river-edge elevations and banks consistent, and avoid displacing a channel into a ridge. Those are valuable when drawing a future M9 river along actual cell outlets and discharge. But its rivers grow from coastal mouths inward on an independently refined triangular mesh, with binary land/sea faces and newly synthesized tributaries. Importing that graph as hydraulic truth would contradict fractional coasts and single-downstream routing. Make invented bends and branches visibly dependent on simulated water; a large blue river cannot be inferred from catchment area alone ([primary PDF §§3–5](https://www.bjoern-ganster.de/pdf/planets.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2011.02052.x)).

**5 — Grenier et al. (2024), “Real-time Terrain Enhancement with Controlled Procedural Patterns,”** offers terrain-adapted phasor noise: steer ravines with the smoothed relief gradient, modulate their amplitude by distance to a drainage cue, add a finer tier and roughen crests. This is a candidate **presentation-only** tile detail conditioned on true outlets, relief and channels, with tests for hypsometric area and tile seams. The paper itself says its patterns cannot generate a globally coherent river network and struggle with flat floodplains, meanders and cliffs. Its breaching comparison is a visual/geomorphic proxy, not a water-budget test; its open PDF is marked CC BY-NC, so code, models and DEM assets require separate commercial-use review ([primary PDF §§3–5](https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/46afc47b-2c2f-4c49-baa3-007eae20b73f/content); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14992)).

**6 — Recent authoring and learned-terrain work is research-only.** Zechmann and Hlavacs’s 2025 Godot comparison offers perceptual-question and LOD/precision ideas, but combines different generators, shaders, controls and environments; its repeated-person questionnaire is analysed with a between-subjects ANOVA, so it cannot rank geological algorithms for this simulator ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24764v1)). Borg et al.’s 2026 terrestrial-planet diffusion paper sketches landcover, temperature, elevation and precipitation and infers detail; the accessible [publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70390) supports an authoring-tool interpretation, **not** a verified deterministic solver or licensed production pipeline. Goslin’s [InfiniteDiffusion paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08309) and [repository](https://github.com/xandergos/terrain-diffusion) offer author-reported seed-consistent hierarchical random access, worth an offline tile-detail comparison; their CUDA/PyTorch models, training rasters and checkpoints have not passed this project’s byte-hash, hypsometry, coast, cost or licensing gates. Generated temperature or precipitation must never enter PlanetSim as climate merely because a model outputs those maps.

## The next acceptance sequence keeps coast and climate separate

**First, decide R1-05 by measurement and ADR amendment, not implementation by analogy.** Freeze seed, sea level, snapshot and coastal focus; compare every visible LOD and transition phase on a common geographic sampling grid in both styles. Measure land/water disagreement, per-cell wet area, shoreline distances in metres and projected pixels, connected components and crossings of shared cell/tile edges; include shelves, steep coasts, islands and narrow straits over multiple seeds and L4–L6. Contrast the two [R1-05](tasks/R1-05-coastline-lod-stability.md) routes: a level-independent georeferenced coast representation with compatible heights/colour, versus registered band-limited relief and morphing. A mask for one sea level need not work after flooding; a smooth morph need not preserve coast topology. The ADR must select a coast definition, numeric displacement/classification gate, transition and storage/latency ceiling **before** production changes. Merely moving the switch, adding skirts or rendering unfiltered detail on coarse tiles does not address the measured semantic jump. Preserve ADR-0022 V4/V9/V10/V11/V13, semantic water extent, PSNAP and state hashes, and compare fixed-camera/blinded same-style zoom clips as a complementary perceptual test.

**Second, run an isolated generator A/B experiment**: current smoothing versus an offline Cordonnier-inspired uplift–drainage–incision pass on the existing cell graph, with fixed seeds and land targets. Record relief and slope distributions, plate-boundary associations, ocean-age/depth relation, shelf profiles, hypsometric curves, depression/spill and basin statistics, outlet catchment closure and river longitudinal profiles against licensed, resolution-matched reference data. Existing [ADR-0005 V1–V9](decisions/0005-coastlines-and-drainage.md) remain mandatory: ordered finite quantiles, monotone connected-ocean fractions, target land area or reported connectivity jump, sea-level bookkeeping, acyclic outlet-reaching drainage, closure, worker-count identity and L5/L6 consistency. Recheck regeneration after load, performance, dead-rock/aqua-planet extremes and climate-relevant surface fractions; only measured improvement and acceptable sensitivity justify adoption. If terrain later evolves *during* gameplay, first decide the cadence, uplift/isostasy, eroded-rock and deposited-sediment stocks, flux closure, field IDs and migration in a separate ADR. An offline shape prior is not a sediment-conserving solver.

**Finally, prototype constrained visual detail** from Derzapf/Grenier only when authoritative M9 discharge and water extent exist for wet rivers; dry geomorphic lines can remain explicitly dry. Verify position-keyed repeatability across workers, traversal and mixed LOD, cell-quantile consistency, no phantom flood/river semantics and unchanged simulation hashes. A diffusion comparison comes later, offline with pinned model/output provenance, asset rights and hard seam/coast/cost tests. Publication code licences do not automatically license weights, training DEMs or redistributed exemplars. The meaningful outcome is not one universal terrain algorithm: it is a documented chain from geological causes to quantiles and water routing, plus a stable, truthful view of that same state.

## Conclusion

The most consequential missing information is **where the visible sea-level contour should lie within a quantile-constrained cell at every zoom and sea level**. Neither nine quantiles nor a clipmap interpolation determine that geography by themselves. Resolve that representation and its testable error budget independently of geomorphic improvements; then let new geology change only the authoritative generation pipeline, and let visual detail remain a faithful consumer of it. This ordering protects both the simulator’s conservation contract and the player’s trust that a coastline, river or flood means the same thing at every scale.
